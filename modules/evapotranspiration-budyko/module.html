1 · Two limits: what the air wants, what the soil can give

Potential evapotranspiration (PET) is the water the atmosphere could take from a surface that never runs short: a demand set by energy (sunlight, temperature, humidity). Actual evapotranspiration (AET) is what really leaves, and it can never be more than the water supplied. Averaged over many years, with no net change in storage, that gives two limits: AET cannot exceed the precipitation P that arrives, and it cannot exceed the demand PET.

Dividing by P puts every basin on the same axes. The aridity index is φ = PET / P and the evaporative index is E / P (E is AET). Over a long average E / P ≤ min(1, φ): a dry basin (φ > 1) is water-limited, and E / P is near 1; a wet basin (φ < 1) is energy-limited, and E / P is near φ. The Budyko curve of §5 runs between those two limits.

Why PET is not AET PET assumes the surface can always supply water. A desert basin has plenty of sun (high PET) but little water, so AET is far below PET. A cool, wet basin has water to spare, so AET is held down by the energy available, not by the water.

2 · PET from temperature alone: Hargreaves–Samani

REAL DATA Pick a basin. Everything below, and in §3, uses its Daymet basin-mean daily weather for water year 1997 (1996-10-01 to 1997-09-30) from CAMELS-US (Newman et al., 2015; Daymet: Thornton et al., 1997).

—

Hargreaves & Samani (1985) need only the daily temperature range and the sunlight that reaches the top of the atmosphere:

PET = 0.0023 · (Ra / λ) · (Tmean + 17.8) · √(Tmax − Tmin)   mm/day, set to 0 if negative
Tmean = (Tmax + Tmin) / 2  ·  λ = 2.45 MJ/kg converts MJ m⁻² day⁻¹ to mm of water

Ra, the extraterrestrial radiation, is the solar energy arriving on a horizontal surface at the top of the atmosphere. It needs no weather data, only latitude φlat and day of year J (the standard geometry tabulated in FAO-56, listed without a DOI in the References):

dr = 1 + 0.033 cos(2πJ/365)  ·  δ = 0.409 sin(2πJ/365 − 1.39)  ·  ωs = arccos(−tan φlat tan δ)
Ra = (24·60/π) · 0.0820 · dr · [ωs sin φlat sin δ + cos φlat cos δ sin ωs]  MJ m⁻² day⁻¹

3 · PET from the energy budget: Priestley–Taylor

Priestley & Taylor (1972) tie evaporation from a wet surface to the net radiation that heats it. Here is the whole calculation for one day of the selected basin, one line per step, built from the Daymet columns: day length, shortwave radiation, Tmax, Tmin and vapour pressure. Choose the day with the slider (or the arrow keys); the table, chart and sentence update together.

—

Assumptions you can see Albedo is held at 0.23 for every day and basin (a stated assumption, not a measurement). The ground heat flux G is taken as 0. The net longwave term uses the clear-sky radiation Rso = (0.75 + 2×10⁻⁵ z) Ra and limits Rs/Rso to 0.3–1 so a very overcast day cannot give a negative longwave loss. Δ is the slope of the saturation vapour-pressure curve at Tmean; γ comes from the elevation z in the forcing-file header.

The Priestley–Taylor coefficient

The coefficient α scales the energy term. The value 1.26 is the one associated with Priestley & Taylor (1972) for a wet surface; applications adjust it. PET here is proportional to α, so every day moves together when you drag it.

4 · AET from the long-term water balance: E = P − Q

  

    Weather gives PET. To get the actual evapotranspiration of a basin, use the mass balance of the catchment: precipitation in, streamflow
    and evapotranspiration out, and a change of storage ΔS in soil, snow and groundwater:
  

  
P − E − Q = ΔS   ⟹   E = P − Q − ΔS   ⟹   E ≈ P − Q  (long-term mean, ΔS → 0)

  

    Over one year ΔS can be large: a wet winter fills the soil, a dry one drains it. Over many years the storage gains and losses average out, so
    E ≈ P − Q, and the evaporative index is E / P = 1 − Q / P, one minus the runoff ratio. The shortcut also assumes the basin is
    closed: no water enters or leaves except as P, E and Q. The bars below use the tabulated long-term means (CAMELS p_mean, q_mean, pet_mean) of the four course basins.

5 · Budyko space: 670 basins and one curve

    its aridity φ = PET / P on the horizontal axis and its evaporative index E / P = 1 − Q / P (§4) on the vertical axis. The dashed lines are the two limits of §1.
    CAMELS tabulates no runoff ratio for gauge 03281100, so 670 of the 671 gauges are plotted.
  

  

    Fu curve (single parameter ω > 1; the form used by Zhang et al., 2004):  E/P = 1 + φ − (1 + φω)1/ω

    ω → 1 gives E/P → 0; as ω grows the curve approaches both limits
  

  

    The shape parameter ω bundles whatever else sets how much of the available water is evaporated: vegetation, soil water storage, the timing of rain relative to energy

    in E/P over every plotted basin.

    Reading points that fall outside the limits
    A point above the limits, or below E / P = 0, is not a violation of physics: E / P here is a derived number, 1 − Q / P, and it carries the assumptions of §4
    (storage change near zero over the average, no water crossing the divide) plus the idea that an annual-mean aridity index captures the climate (snow storage and out-of-phase seasons break that).
    A point outside the limits therefore says those assumptions do not hold for that basin and period. This lesson counts such basins; it does not decide which assumption fails. Possible reasons, none tested here: water that crosses the divide underground, records that cover different periods, a short record, or a gauged area that differs from the area the forcing describes. The Siletz River point (E/P near 0.01 in the cell output of §5) is the kind of point this note is about.

Real data (§2–§5)
CAMELS-US v1.2: Daymet basin-mean daily dayl, srad, tmax, tmin and vp for water year 1997 for four basins; aridity, runoff_ratio and frac_snow for 671 gauges; p_mean, q_mean and pet_mean for the four. Source members and sha256 in data/PROVENANCE.json. Table values are rounded to four decimals (recorded there).

  
PET methods
Hargreaves–Samani and Priestley–Taylor as written in §2–§3; constants: λ 2.45 MJ/kg, albedo 0.23 (assumption), α default 1.26, G = 0. Not used: any model-output PET.

  
Budyko fit
Least squares on E/P over a grid ω = 1.100…6.000, step 0.001, n = 670 basins (gauge 03281100 has no runoff_ratio).

Python (numpy, matplotlib) via AI-Hydro preview kernel.
